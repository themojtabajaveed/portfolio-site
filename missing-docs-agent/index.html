The practice's problem

"What are we
still waiting for?"

A client sends some receipts, a few invoices and most of their bank statements. Someone still has to match them against the job, spot the gaps and write another email.

We separate the reading from the rules: AI extracts the fields, a deterministic checklist finds missing or unclear items, and a human checks the draft.

This is a demonstration of the workflow, not evidence of saved staff hours or improved client response rates. Those need to be measured in a pilot.

01 / Four steps, end to end

From intake to human review.

01 / Intake + extraction

Read what arrived

The local pipeline reads a synthetic client folder. Providers classify documents and extract type, date, total and counterparty. The live LLM route uses Gemini, a backup Gemini key and Groq for text fallback.

Boundary: this is folder-based input, not a deployed client upload portal. The offline baseline is regex, not AI.

02 / Checklist + flags

Find the specific gaps

Rules for sole traders, limited-company year ends and VAT packs compare received documents with the client profile. Results distinguish complete, missing and unclear, including missing statement months.

Checks: separate validation flags wrong year, wrong client and non-business documents. Rule sources cite public gov.uk guidance; an accountant must confirm the checklist.

03 / Draft + dashboard

Ask only for what is missing

Deterministic templates turn missing or unclear items into a polite chase email. The Streamlit dashboard brings the client checklist and draft into one review screen.

Boundary: chase copy is template-based, not LLM-written. The dashboard uses synthetic records.

04 / Approval + audit

A person approves. Nothing sends.

A named reviewer approves the draft. The prototype writes a local outbox entry and approval audit trail, then calculates reminder dates at day 3, 7 and 14.

Boundary: no email is sent. Reminder logic can re-check remaining items; a live scheduler, mail integration and database-backed audit trail are not verified.

The test set

The current harness targets 53 labelled synthetic documents: clean text, British date formats, OCR damage, photo-like images, wrong-year and wrong-client documents, and junk.

The final published report must state what the rerun actually attempted. This is not a real-client study or a guarantee of production accuracy.

What counts as a miss

Type and date must match the labelled answer. Totals must match within half a penny. Counterparty matching normalises case, punctuation and certain company suffixes.

The harness excludes fields with no labelled value. Skips and request failures must be reported alongside scores, not hidden behind a percentage.

04 / Proposed pilot

Two weeks.
One client segment.

A possible pilot shape, to agree with your practice. No rollout, pricing or real-data processing is committed by this page.

  • Choose one client segment and agree its document checklist with a qualified accountant.
  • Start with synthetic documents, then agree data-processing terms before using real client files.
  • Set the human-review and sending process. The current prototype never sends.
  • Measure missed items, corrections and time spent reviewing. Keep a record of failures.
  • At two weeks, decide whether to continue, change the workflow or stop.

Hosting, access, retention, deletion, provider use and international transfers must be agreed first. No GDPR compliance or ICO registration claim is made here.
